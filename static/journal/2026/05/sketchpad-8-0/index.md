Sketchpad 8.0 is taking shape as the most focused release of Sketchpad in years. The goal is not to make the app louder. It is to make the whole drawing surface feel calmer, faster, and more direct.

Earlier versions of Sketchpad grew by adding tools, panels, and formats. That history matters. It is why the app can move between quick sketches, diagrams, classroom projects, social graphics, and more polished design work. But a mature creative tool also needs moments where the surface gets simpler again.

This release is about that reset.

The work in 8.0 centers on three ideas:

- A cleaner workspace that keeps the canvas first.
- Faster everyday actions, especially around selecting, editing, arranging, and exporting.
- A steadier foundation for the next wave of drawing, layout, and asset workflows.

The best version of Sketchpad should feel approachable in the first minute and still useful after years of making things with it. That means fewer surprises, clearer controls, and an interface that gets out of the way without hiding the power underneath.

Sketchpad has always lived somewhere between drawing app, design tool, classroom utility, and creative playground. Version 8.0 leans into that mix with a little more restraint. The app should still feel playful, but the frame around the work should feel more deliberate.

There is still more to polish before this goes live, but the direction feels right: less friction, more canvas, and a better path for everything Sketchpad becomes next.
