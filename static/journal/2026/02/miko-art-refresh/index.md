If a website is a room, this one finally feels like a place I want to stay in.

Over the last stretch, I rebuilt major parts of MIKO.ART with one simple goal: make it easier to read, easier to browse, and easier to trust. The old version had heart, but it also had rough edges. Different page widths, different pacing, and different visual rhythms made the experience feel fragmented.

The new version is still playful, still me, but now it has a clearer system underneath it.

## What changed

The biggest shift is consistency.

Page widths are now aligned across key routes, so you no longer jump between narrow and wide layouts while browsing. The sitemap, labs, and journal now share a common visual language: clear controls, tidy sections, and metadata that is easy to scan.

I also gave journal reading pages a full typography pass. Better spacing, stronger hierarchy, calmer line lengths, and cleaner image treatment make posts feel less like raw markdown and more like a modern editorial page.

In short: less visual noise, more reading flow.

## Designed for flow, not just style

I love colorful interfaces, but color without structure is just decoration. This refresh is intentionally quieter in the right places.

- Navigation and controls now feel predictable.
- Content surfaces are cleaner and more readable.
- Metadata is present but not shouting.
- Mobile behavior is tighter and more graceful.

The effect is subtle until you spend a few minutes with it. Then you notice it: fewer tiny frictions, less cognitive load, more momentum.

## The new journal direction

The journal is where this update matters most.

Listing pages now feel more like a map: searchable, filterable, and grouped so you can jump around by intent instead of scrolling blindly. Individual entries now have a cleaner "modern newspaper" feel: generous type, stronger rhythm, and a layout that lets writing breathe.

I want these posts to feel like reading, not "consuming content."

## Why this matters

A personal site should not feel like an archive you endure. It should feel like a studio you can walk through.

This refresh brings MIKO.ART closer to that idea. It is still a mix of code, art, and experiments, but now the container better respects the work inside it.

Thanks for reading, and thanks to everyone who kept nudging me toward "one more polish pass." You were right.
